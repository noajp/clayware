We help you rework how the job is done, on the assumption that AI is part of it.

Our engineers work on site with your team, from mapping the work, setting success criteria and connecting AI, through trial runs, to settling the new way into daily operations.

Common situations, and what we do first

A common situationWhat we do first
AI was introduced but is used only to draft textRe-divide the work, process by process, into what AI does and what people judge
Requests are split across mail and chat, and some are missedConnect the services requests arrive in to Clayware, and record every request with an owner and a deadline
No one can tell whose decision the work is waiting onList only the work that needs a person’s judgment, with who it waits on and for how long
Only the person doing the job knows the stepsWrite the steps and the conditions to stop into a document AI can also read (a harness)

Four stages

  1. 1. Study the work on site

    List where requests come from, who decides what, and how long each part takes.

  2. 2. Set success criteria

    Decide in numbers whether the work is going well, such as “first reply within 4 hours”.

  3. 3. Connect and run a trial

    Connect Clayware to the work and run it with writes to outside services stopped, checking every operation AI performs.

  4. 4. Fix while operating

    Each morning, check the list of decisions waiting and the results, and fix the steps against the success criteria.

How much is handed to AI

In Clayware you choose, from three levels, whether AI may run operations that write to outside services without approval.

Always askEvery write to an outside service waits for a person’s approval. This is where you start after introduction.
AutomaticOperations that ran without being sent back are allowed without approval, one by one. A send-back or an incident narrows the range again.
Don’t askOperations within the allowed range run without approval. Operations that can’t be undone still wait for approval.

Three roles in the engagement

  1. EngineersOur side

    Study the work, connect Clayware to your services, write the harnesses, and run trials and fixes.

  2. People doing the workYour side

    Ask AI for work, check the results and send back what doesn’t fit. Tell the engineers what is hard to use.

  3. Managers and ownersWho decides

    Set the success criteria and which operations need approval, and decide from the results whether to hand over more.
